June 2025 · Gerald and Sheila or Power

8702/2 · Choose one question

Question 01 · Gerald and Sheila

How does Priestley present Gerald and Sheila’s changing relationship in An Inspector Calls?

Write about:

  • Gerald and Sheila’s relationship in the play
  • how Priestley presents Gerald and Sheila’s changing relationship.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Compare the celebration and the returned ring with Gerald offering it again. Is the ending a reconciliation, or do the two now want different things?

One possible argument · The engagement begins as reassurance of social and family stability, but Sheila’s refusal to settle the question at the end shows how differently the two understand what has happened.

Question 02 · Power

How does Priestley present ideas about power in An Inspector Calls?

Write about:

  • some of the ideas about power that are shown in the play
  • how Priestley presents ideas about power.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Consider how employers, customers and charity committees exercise different kinds of power. What changes when the Inspector takes control of the room?

One possible argument · Priestley makes institutional power visible through Eva’s limited options, then shifts the power onstage to a visitor who controls what the family can see and when.

June 2024 · Mrs Birling and class or The Inspector and social change

8702/2 · Choose one question

Question 01 · Mrs Birling and class

How does Priestley use the character of Mrs Birling to explore ideas about social class?

Write about:

  • what Mrs Birling says and does
  • how Priestley presents Mrs Birling.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Compare how she judges Eva’s use of the Birling name with how she responds to Eric. Is class a principle for her, or a means of protecting her position?

One possible argument · Mrs Birling’s authority rests on social distinction, but the revelation about Eric exposes the limits of the moral judgements she makes on that basis.

Question 02 · The Inspector and social change

How does Priestley use the Inspector to suggest the need for social change in An Inspector Calls?

Write about:

  • what the Inspector says and does
  • how Priestley presents the Inspector.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Compare his questioning of individuals with his final address. What can the audience take from the final phone call even if his identity remains uncertain?

One possible argument · The Inspector turns an apparently private investigation into a public argument about responsibility, and the final call keeps that argument alive after he leaves.

June 2023 · Women’s lives or Generational responses

8702/2 · Choose one question

Question 01 · Women’s lives

How does Priestley present what life is like for women in An Inspector Calls?

Write about:

  • what life is shown to be like for women in the play
  • how Priestley presents what life is like for women.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Compare Eva’s limited choices with Sheila’s growing independence. Where does class make a greater difference than gender alone can explain?

One possible argument · Priestley shows that women’s choices are constrained by both class and gender: Sheila can come to recognise injustice, while Eva’s efforts to resist have material consequences.

Question 02 · Generational responses

How does Priestley present the different ways older and younger characters respond to the Inspector?

Write about:

  • the different ways older and younger characters respond to the Inspector
  • how Priestley presents the different ways older and younger characters respond to the Inspector.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Compare the family’s reactions after the Inspector leaves. Does Gerald fit neatly into either group, and what does that do to your argument?

One possible argument · Sheila and Eric accept moral responsibility independently of the Inspector’s official status, while their parents are more willing to treat the absence of proof as absolution.

June 2022 · Eric and society or Inequality and tragedy

8702/1M · Choose one question

Question 01 · Eric and society

How far does Priestley present Eric as a character who learns important lessons about society in An Inspector Calls?

Write about:

  • what Eric says and does in the play
  • how Priestley presents Eric.

[30 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Look at how Eric talks about money, his parents and Eva after the revelations. Which change shows a broader understanding, and which might be personal remorse?

One possible argument · Eric begins to see why having money and status gives him obligations, although his new awareness arrives too late to help Eva.

Question 02 · Inequality and tragedy

‘Priestley shows how inequality in society leads to tragedy.’

How far do you agree with this view of An Inspector Calls?

Write about:

  • what happens in the play
  • how Priestley presents what happens in the play.

[30 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Follow Eva from employment to the charity committee. Which losses of choice come from social position, and where do individual decisions still matter?

One possible argument · The play treats inequality as a system that makes Eva vulnerable to repeated setbacks, while showing the choices through which individuals sustain that system.

November 2021 · Gerald and responsibility or An unfair society

8702/1M · Choose one question

Question 01 · Gerald and responsibility

How does Priestley use Gerald to explore ideas about responsibility?

Write about:

  • what Gerald says and does
  • how Priestley uses Gerald to explore ideas about responsibility.

[30 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Compare his account of helping Daisy with his later efforts to establish the Inspector’s identity. Is accepting responsibility the same as admitting what happened?

One possible argument · Gerald shows that taking practical action for somebody can coexist with using power over them, and that telling the truth can fall short of accepting lasting responsibility.

Question 02 · An unfair society

How far does Priestley present society as unfair in An Inspector Calls?

Write about:

  • what can be seen as unfair in the play
  • how far Priestley presents society as unfair.

[30 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Set the opening celebration against Eva’s situation. Who gets to decide whether she deserves work, shelter or help, and how is that inequality staged?

One possible argument · Eva is repeatedly judged by people insulated from the consequences of their own decisions, making unfairness visible in everyday institutions.

November 2020 · Mr Birling and self-interest or Irresponsible men

8702/2 · Choose one question

Question 01 · Mr Birling and self-interest

Mr Birling says, ‘…a man has to mind his own business and look after himself and his own’.

How far does Priestley present Mr Birling as a man who cares only for himself and his family?

Write about:

  • what Mr Birling says and does
  • how far Priestley presents Mr Birling as a man who cares only for himself and his family.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Compare Birling’s advice at dinner with what he wants to protect after the inquiry. Is he defending his family, his reputation, or both?

One possible argument · Birling’s desire to protect his family frequently overlaps with his desire to preserve his standing; when those interests conflict, the play tests how generous his concern really is.

Question 02 · Irresponsible men

How far does Priestley present male characters as irresponsible in the play?

Write about:

  • one or more of the male character(s)
  • how far Priestley presents one or more of the male character(s) as irresponsible.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Compare Birling, Gerald and Eric. Where do they use their position to avoid consequences, and which of them is willing to live with what he has learned?

One possible argument · The men use position, money and reputation to avoid accountability in different ways, but Eric’s response at the end makes the group less uniform.

June 2018 · Eric’s change or Social class

8702/2 · Choose one question

Question 01 · Eric’s change

How far does Priestley present Eric as a character who changes his attitudes towards himself and others during the play?

Write about:

  • what Eric says and does throughout the play
  • how far Priestley presents Eric as a character who changes his attitudes.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Compare his evasiveness at dinner with his willingness to face responsibility at the end. What can a change in attitude repair, and what can it never undo?

One possible argument · Eric moves from discomfort and evasion towards accepting his part in what happened, although the play leaves the practical value of that change open.

Question 02 · Social class

How does Priestley explore the importance of social class in An Inspector Calls?

Write about:

  • some ideas about social class in the play
  • how Priestley presents the importance of social class.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Who can order, dismiss or refuse someone, and who cannot answer back? Consider why Priestley places those decisions in a comfortable dining room.

One possible argument · Social class determines who can exercise judgement and who has to endure its effects, and Priestley’s dining-room setting keeps that imbalance in view.

June 2017 · Mrs Birling or The Inspector’s proposals

8702/2 · Choose one question

Question 01 · Mrs Birling

How far does Priestley present Mrs Birling as an unlikeable character?

Write about:

  • what Mrs Birling says and does in the play
  • how Priestley presents her by the ways he writes.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Compare her confidence at the charity committee with the revelation that follows her judgement of the father. Can you explain her certainty without endorsing it?

One possible argument · Mrs Birling’s unexamined certainty can repel an audience, especially when her judgement rebounds on her own family.

Question 02 · The Inspector’s proposals

How does Priestley use the character of the Inspector to suggest ways that society could be improved?

Write about:

  • what society is shown to be like in the play and how it might be improved
  • how Priestley presents society through what the Inspector says and does.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Look at his questioning and final speech. How does the contrast with Birling’s opening advice invite the audience to choose between two ways of living?

One possible argument · The Inspector argues for social improvement by replacing Birling’s ethic of individual self-interest with the idea that choices impose obligations to others.

Question 01 · Sheila’s development

How and why does Sheila change in An Inspector Calls?

Write about:

  • how Sheila responds to her family and to the Inspector
  • how Priestley presents Sheila by the ways he writes.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Compare Sheila’s words at the engagement, during the inquiry and after it. How does she move from being questioned to questioning her family?

One possible argument · The investigation changes Sheila from a participant in an engagement celebration into someone who can interrogate the assumptions supporting that celebration.

Question 02 · Responsibility

How does Priestley explore responsibility in An Inspector Calls?

Write about:

  • the ideas about responsibility in An Inspector Calls
  • how Priestley presents these ideas by the ways he writes.

[30 marks] · AO4 [4 marks]

Thinking prompt and example argument

Consider why the family’s relief before the final phone call matters. What changes if responsibility depends on proof rather than on what they did?

One possible argument · The ending tests whether responsibility depends on the authority of the person asking the questions, or on the actions already admitted.

Read the play by act

  1. Act 1 · The engagement dinner gives way to the Inspector’s inquiry. Birling and Sheila describe how their decisions affected Eva.
  2. Act 2 · Gerald explains his relationship with Daisy. Mrs Birling defends the charity’s decision, then learns who the father is.
  3. Act 3 · Eric describes his actions. After the Inspector leaves, the family argues over what his identity changes. A phone call reopens the inquiry.
